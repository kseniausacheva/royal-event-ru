Partner Cooperation & Service Standards Agreement

This draft is intended for guides, venues, transport providers, production teams, destination partners and other business suppliers working with La Royal Event in Egypt and on agreed international projects.

The proposed agreement is between LA ROYAL EVENT for Commercial Chains LLC (Arabic registered name: لارويال ايفنت للسلاسل التجارية), an Egyptian limited liability company trading as La Royal Event, Commercial Register No. 217967 at the Cairo Investment Commercial Registry Office, Tax ID 756-970-857, with its registered office at Apartment 24, Building 11, Group 74, Phase 7, Madinaty, New Cairo, Cairo, Egypt, represented by Ksenia Usacheva and Ekaterina Gaiduk, acting jointly as its managers (the Company), and the business or professional identified in the accepted partner application (the Partner). Together they are the Parties.

1. Purpose and relationship

1.1 The Parties wish to explore cooperation on corporate events, meetings, incentives, conferences, travel programmes and related services. These terms set standards for discussions, information sharing and agreed assignments.

1.2 Cooperation intentions create no obligation to place or accept orders, guarantee work, grant exclusivity or conclude a future contract. The confidentiality, data protection, brand, client protection and other expressly stated obligations are intended to bind the Parties once the final agreement is validly accepted.

1.3 Each assignment requires a separate written contract, purchase order or statement of work accepted by authorised representatives. It must identify the services, dates, responsibilities, price, currency, applicable taxes, payment, cancellation, insurance, permits and any project-specific liability terms. No booking or expenditure is authorised by this agreement alone.

1.4 The Partner acts as an independent business or professional. It cannot bind the Company, promise refunds, vary a programme or incur costs in the Company's name without written authority. This agreement does not create an employment relationship, legal partnership or general agency.

2. Definitions and authorised contacts

2.1 A Client is a guest, corporate customer, agency or other customer introduced to the Partner by the Company for an identified project or opportunity. A Protected Contact is a Client or supplier whose non-public contact details or commercial relationship the Partner first receives through that introduction. Documented pre-existing relationships are excluded.

2.2 Confidential Information is non-public information disclosed for cooperation that is marked confidential or reasonably understood to be confidential, including budgets, rates, proposals, guest details, security arrangements, itineraries, supplier terms, credentials and internal materials. Public website content is not confidential merely because the Company published it.

2.3 The Partner must identify its legal entity or professional status, authorised representative, operational contact and emergency contact accurately, and promptly notify the Company of relevant changes, conflicts of interest or any loss of a required licence or insurance. Project instructions must come from the Company's designated manager.

3. Confidentiality

3.1 Each Party must use the other's Confidential Information only to assess or perform agreed cooperation; protect it with reasonable technical and organisational measures; and disclose it only to people who need it for that purpose and are bound by appropriate confidentiality obligations. Copies must be limited to operational need.

3.2 The Partner must restrict access to project files and credentials, use secure transfer methods and prevent unauthorised access, copying or publication. It must notify the Company without undue delay if Confidential Information is lost, misused or accessed without authority, and assist with containment.

3.3 These restrictions do not cover information demonstrably already held lawfully, independently developed, received lawfully without restriction, or public without breach. A legally required disclosure must be limited to what is required; give advance notice where legally permitted.

3.4 On request or when no longer needed, return or securely delete the information, except records required by law or needed for a bona fide legal claim. Restricted retained records remain protected and must not be used for marketing.

4. White-label delivery and brand use

4.1 For each assignment the Partner must follow the agreed branding instructions, whether La Royal Event, the corporate client's brand or another approved identity. The Partner must not present its own marketing material, business cards, QR codes or promotional links to guests without the Company's written approval.

4.2 Any permission to use names, logos or materials is limited to the approved project and purpose. Ownership remains with the relevant rights holder. The Partner must stop unauthorised use promptly and must not imply a wider endorsement or continuing affiliation.

4.3 White-label presentation must never conceal legally required supplier identification, licences, safety information or the identity of a responsible operator. The Partner must not misrepresent its legal status or authority.

5. Client relationships and non-circumvention

5.1 During cooperation and for 12 months after the relevant introduction or last project involving the Protected Contact, whichever is later, the Partner must not knowingly use the Company's introduction or Confidential Information to divert that project, bypass the Company in obtaining substantially related work, or arrange such conduct through people acting on its behalf.

5.2 The Partner must not sell guests unapproved extras, solicit private bookings, harvest guest contacts or disclose project contacts for such purposes. Requests outside the agreed scope must be referred promptly to the designated manager. Routine operational communication needed to deliver the project is permitted.

5.3 This restriction does not prohibit documented pre-existing business, general advertising not targeted using the Company's information, independently obtained opportunities or work expressly approved in writing. Any concern about an existing relationship must be disclosed before the assignment. The restriction applies only to the extent permitted by applicable law.

6. Personal data and information security

6.1 Each Party must comply with the personal data laws applicable to its activities, including Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020 and applicable implementing requirements where relevant. Before transferring guest or staff data, the Parties must confirm their roles, lawful basis, required notices, permissions and any necessary data-processing or international-transfer arrangements.

6.2 When acting on the Company's instructions, the Partner may process only the minimum data needed for the specified service. Access must be limited to authorised personnel under confidentiality obligations. Appropriate measures include strong access controls, protected devices, secure transmission and a retention schedule.

6.3 The Partner must not reuse data for advertising, personal contact lists, profiling or unrelated purposes. It must not appoint another processor, disclose data or transfer it to another country without the required written authorisation and lawful arrangements. Health, biometric, passport and children's data require a separate assessment; they must not be collected through the general partner application.

6.4 Notify the Company without undue delay and, where practicable, within 24 hours of becoming aware of a suspected personal data incident. Provide known facts, protective measures and updates. This contractual notification does not replace any statutory duty or deadline. Assist with rights requests, lawful investigations and secure return or deletion, while preserving legally required records.

7. Photography, publicity and intellectual property

7.1 Guest photography, recordings, interviews, testimonials, behind-the-scenes material and project case studies require prior project approval and all necessary individual permissions and location permits. The Company's approval does not replace a guest's rights or a third party's permission.

7.2 No project-related material may be posted on social media, websites, messaging channels, portfolios or presentations without written approval covering the intended use. This includes confidential budgets, guest lists, security arrangements and internal discussions.

7.3 Each Party retains rights in its pre-existing materials. Ownership, licence scope, payment and permitted use of commissioned photographs, films, designs and other deliverables must be stated in the project contract, including necessary third-party releases. No blanket transfer of every work created is assumed under this framework.

8. Professional conduct, safety and delivery

8.1 The Partner must provide competent, appropriately licensed personnel, suitable equipment and any insurance required for its activities or project. It must obtain the permits allocated to it, follow approved timings and specifications, and disclose material limitations before accepting work.

8.2 Treat guests and colleagues respectfully. Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, intoxication while on duty and conduct that compromises safety are unacceptable. Discuss sensitive cultural, religious or political subjects professionally only where relevant to the agreed programme.

8.3 Raise delays, shortages, safety concerns and complaints promptly with the designated manager. Protect life and safety first: contact emergency services when necessary and do not wait for commercial approval to take reasonable urgent protective action. Do not conceal an incident or discourage lawful reporting.

8.4 Do not pressure guests for tips, redirect them to shops for undisclosed commissions, accept hidden referral payments or offer unapproved paid extras. Disclose conflicts and commercial incentives. No bribes, facilitation payments or unlawful benefits may be offered or accepted.

8.5 Changes, substitutions and subcontracting require the agreed written approval except necessary emergency action. The Partner remains responsible for the people it engages and must ensure equivalent relevant standards. Financial discussions and internal disagreements should be handled with the manager, without misleading guests or obstructing legitimate complaints.

9. Commercial terms and responsibility

9.1 Fees, deposits, refunds, bank details and cancellation charges belong in the individual project contract or verified invoice. Applicable taxes and invoice requirements must be assessed for that supply; a foreign customer or foreign-currency payment does not by itself determine tax treatment.

9.2 Each Party is responsible for breaches attributable to it under applicable law and the relevant project contract. This framework imposes no fixed contractual fine. Any agreed damages, limitation of liability or indemnity requires an express project provision and remains subject to mandatory law.

9.3 The Company may stop discussions or suspend a Partner's involvement where a material breach, credible safety concern or unauthorised disclosure requires it. Where practical, give the Partner a reasonable opportunity to respond or remedy. Suspension does not itself cancel valid payment obligations for properly performed work; project cancellation and force-majeure consequences follow the project contract and applicable law.

10. Duration, ending cooperation and survival

10.1 The final agreement starts when accepted under section 12 and continues until either Party ends it by written notice. Ending the framework does not automatically cancel an existing project contract. Any transition must protect guests, property, confidential information and outstanding obligations.

10.2 Confidentiality continues for three years after termination, and for trade secrets for as long as they remain lawfully protected. Data protection obligations continue for as long as personal data is retained. Section 5 applies for its stated period; intellectual property restrictions and accrued rights survive as necessary.

11. Governing law and general terms

11.1 The final agreement is intended to be governed by the laws of the Arab Republic of Egypt, subject to applicable mandatory rules. The Parties should first seek a good-faith resolution through their authorised representatives. The competent forum and any agreed dispute procedure must be completed in the final version: [DISPUTE FORUM TO BE CONFIRMED].

11.2 Changes require mutual written agreement or a clearly presented new version accepted for future cooperation. Updating a website alone does not retrospectively change an accepted agreement. A project contract prevails over this framework for an expressly identified inconsistency.

11.3 If a provision is unenforceable, the remaining terms apply to the extent legally possible. No waiver arises solely from a delayed response. The intended controlling language is English, without limiting mandatory Arabic documentation or court translation requirements.

12. Electronic application and acceptance

12.1 This draft cannot be accepted. The live process may begin only after the Company identifies itself fully, publishes a final dated version and provides the applicable privacy notice. A partner application alone does not create an order or confirm approved supplier status.

12.2 In the intended final process, the Partner's authorised representative provides accurate business and contact details, opens the final agreement, actively ticks an unticked acceptance box identifying its version, confirms authority and submits the form. Identity or authority checks and email verification may be required.

12.3 The Company will retain the accepted text or immutable version reference, submitted identity and authority information, acceptance choices, timestamp, verification result and an application reference. Technical records may be retained only as disclosed in the privacy notice and permitted by law. The Partner must receive a copy or durable link and confirmation.

12.4 The final workflow must state precisely when agreement formation occurs and distinguish it from later supplier approval. Electronic records may support evidence of intention and consent, subject to applicable law; this framework does not declare an ordinary checkbox or email to be a certified electronic signature. A signed counterpart may be used where required.
